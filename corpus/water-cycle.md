# The water cycle

The water cycle, also called the hydrologic cycle, describes the continuous
movement of water on, above and below the surface of the Earth. The total
amount of water on the planet stays roughly constant; what changes is where
that water sits and what state it is in.

## Evaporation

Solar energy heats water in oceans, lakes and rivers, turning liquid water into
water vapour. Oceans supply roughly 86 percent of global evaporation. Plants
contribute additional vapour through transpiration, releasing water through
pores in their leaves. Together these two processes are called
evapotranspiration.

## Condensation

As water vapour rises it cools. Cooler air holds less moisture, so the vapour
condenses around tiny particles of dust, salt or smoke, forming the droplets
that make up clouds. The altitude at which this begins is called the lifting
condensation level.

## Precipitation

When droplets in a cloud collide and merge they grow heavier until air currents
can no longer hold them aloft. They then fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail
depending on the temperature profile of the air they pass through.

## Collection and runoff

Precipitation that reaches the ground either soaks into the soil, becoming
groundwater, or flows across the surface as runoff into streams and rivers,
eventually returning to the ocean. Groundwater can remain underground for
thousands of years before re-emerging, a period known as its residence time.
